Summarizing my Findings

For this project, I was mainly interested in how specific variables, such as weekly study time, internet access, travel time to school, family relationships, and more influence a student’s performance on their math test scores as I plan to become a future math teacher.

I first found that the weekly study time of 5 to 10 hours resulted in the highest average final grade. I found this quite interesting as it had a higher average than over 10 hours of studying. Students who participated in extracurricular activities had a higher final grade compared to students who did not participate. There was a drastic difference in the average final grade compared to a student’s interest in higher education, where students who said they had an interest had a greater average final grade.

One thing I found quite weird was the students who had the lowest reported health level performed usually greater on the final grade score. I did not really see a student’s family relationship or free time level affecting the average final grade score that much. However, the travel time to school had a big impact on the final grade score as the farther away a student was, the lower the final grade was. Internet access did result in a higher average final grade and the smaller number of absences results in usually a higher final grade.

This project can overall help me better understand these patterns and aid me in becoming a more effective future math teacher by showing that student performance may be influenced by outside factors that students might or might not have control over. This puts emphasis on that students may enter a classroom with different experiences and levels of background and support. Knowing this can help me plan lessons that can provide clear instruction and multiple ways for students to learn so all students can have the opportunity to succeed no matter what their background is. It also shows the importance of encouraging students helpful study habits and overall creating a classroom environment where every student is capable of succeeding.

This dashboard displays summary statistics for students’ performance in a math course from two Portuguese schools. The data was collected from school reports and questionnaires. This project explores how students’ math final grades change based on many different variables.

The variables I chose to look at in the dataset are:

  • Travel Time: how long it takes a student to travel from home to school

  • Weekly Study Time: how much time a student studies each week

  • Activities: whether a student participates in extracurricular activities

  • Higher Education Interest: whether a student has interest in pursuing higher education

  • Internet Access: whether a student has internet access at home

  • Family Relationships: the quality of a student’s family relationships, going from 1 which is very low to 5 which is very high

  • Free Time: how much a student has free time after school, going from 1 which is very low to 5 which is very high

  • Health: a student’s current health status, going from 1 which is very low to 5 which is very high

  • Absences: the number of school absences a student has

  • First Semester Grade: the student’s first grade score in the course, ranging from 0 to 20

  • Second Semester Grade: the student’s second grade score in the course, ranging from 0 to 20

  • Final Grade: the student’s final grade in the course, ranging from 0 to 20

In this dashboard, the Overview page summarizes the dataset and shows the average first semester, second semester, and final grades. The Academic Factors, Personal Factors, and Outside Factor pages compare final grades towards different variables related to student performance and overall well being. The Conclusion page summarizes the findings from the dataset and the Data page shows the full dataset.
